Reactive thermal control fires after the silicon has already touched the trip point. coolstep looks ahead — learns the workload's fingerprint, predicts the peak in the next 30 s, lifts the fan curve and boost cap before the chip warms.

  1. 01 Anticipate When the predictor sees a load peak in the next 30 s, fans get a head start and the boost cap lifts before the silicon heats. When load actually arrives, RPM is already on target — no impeller inertia, no heatsink soak delay. The mirror trick on the way down: drop the boost cap as soon as load fades, before the curve has to chase a cooling temperature.
  2. 02 Why it works Sub-threshold leakage scales exponentially with T — every °C of silicon multiplies static current (Arrhenius-form on the trap-emission rate; Poole-Frenkel under field). A pre-cooled chip on the same workload draws less, heats less, demands less cooling — a self-reinforcing loop. Spinning the fan up before the load arrives = keeping the silicon in its efficient regime instead of climbing the leakage-cliff.
